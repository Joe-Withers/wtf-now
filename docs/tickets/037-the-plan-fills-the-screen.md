# 037: The plan fills the screen

**Depends on**: 036

The plan was a tree of cards down the left of the screen, in a column about 330px wide. On a laptop that left most of the screen empty — the last of the "loads of whitespace on the left and right" that the redesign was meant to answer.

Now the plan lays itself out to the space it is given:

- The plan measures the space it has, and works out how many columns of work fit in it — one on a phone, two on a small laptop, three on a big one.
- The top-level groups are split across the columns, so each group and everything under it stays together. The columns are about the same height, so the plan reads evenly rather than as one long list and one stub.
- The cards grow to fill their column, up to a sensible maximum, so there are no dead strips down the side.
- The drawing is centred in the space, so the space is shared either side rather than all on one side.
- A group and the parts under it still hang from one another with the same elbow lines, and a picked task still shows what it needs and what it holds up.

The screen is now called **Plan**, like the other screens, rather than showing the wordmark again — the rail already has the wordmark on it.

**Done when**: the plan on a laptop fills the screen with work instead of sitting in a narrow strip down one side.
